# Competitive positioning

**Research date:** 2026-11-05

**Scope:** Public official vendor product, candidate-help, integration, and pricing pages. Vendor capability descriptions below are attributed claims, not independently tested results. I did not create accounts, run assessments, time onboarding, or inspect candidate sessions. Price pages can change; all figures are USD as displayed, before tax.

## Current Hirearchy boundary

The current PR #5 commit is an internal staging foundation, not the complete buyer journey. Its authenticated recruiter path does not issue candidate links, produce candidate activity, or allow evidence and human-review writes; it also does not establish self-service organization onboarding or a production deployment. The readiness record is the source for the exact commit, checks, and release gates: [MVP release readiness](../release-readiness.md). The positioning below describes the intended MVP after integration, not features available to buyers today.

## Positioning answers

1. **Why choose Hirearchy?** The intended choice is a realistic engineering task paired with an inspectable, chronological account of what the candidate did, the sources behind each event, and a human-owned review. This is most relevant when the buyer wants to understand how someone approached work instead of relying on a score alone. The whole journey has not yet been demonstrated on the current release path.
2. **Which hiring pain could it solve better?** Hypothesis: hiring managers spend scarce engineering time reviewing take-home work, but often receive only a final artifact, a score, or a short replay. Hirearchy could make the candidate’s work and its evidence easier to inspect and discuss. Public competitor pages show that session replay and code playback already exist, so “we have a replay” is not enough differentiation. Validate the cost and frequency of this review problem with buyers before making a stronger claim.
3. **What can competitors do that Hirearchy cannot today?** Current public competitors offer mature question libraries, automatic scoring, quick email invitations, candidate practice/preparation, integrated live interviews, ATS connections, and several forms of proctoring/reporting. PR #5 does not yet issue candidate invitations or support candidate completion, evidence creation, or saved evaluator review in staging. These are real product gaps, not merely marketing gaps.
4. **What is genuinely differentiated?** The product thesis is evidence provenance across a realistic engineering workflow, with observations kept separate from evaluator judgment and AI use treated as neutral. This is a differentiation hypothesis, not a proven market advantage: several vendors already show code playback, session recordings, scores, or integrity events. The distinction must be visible in the complete workflow and useful in buyer evaluation.
5. **Which expected table stakes are missing?** The immediate gaps are a working recruiter-to-candidate invitation, clear candidate notice/consent, recoverable save/resume/submit, evaluator assignment and evidence access, retained human review, secure revocation/finality, self-service or explicitly supported first-team setup, and a supported deploy/recovery path. Do not add webcam surveillance merely to match competitors; offer clear, proportionate integrity evidence and human review.
6. **What belongs in the MVP?** One polished end-to-end software-engineering task: recruiter signs in and issues an expiring, revocable copy link; candidate understands data use, works, saves/resumes, and submits; the assigned evaluator sees factual evidence and records a human review. Make failure, persistence, and completion states honest. Keep the durable internal demo free of live AI, external email, and code execution; code execution must separately pass the external-beta security and operations gate.
7. **What should be postponed, and what is excluded?** ATS integrations, broad role/language coverage, large question catalogs, billing automation, advanced analytics, webcam/biometric monitoring, and live AI provider activation can wait until the core workflow is proven. Candidate scores or rankings, competence labels/percentages, and automatic pass/reject/hire verdicts are **excluded by current product rules**; they are not P2 features or a deferred roadmap.
8. **What initial commercial model is worth testing?** The internal pricing hypothesis is a **US$250 fixed, 30-day design-partner pilot** for one team, one approved scenario, and up to 10 candidate sessions, credited toward a future small-team plan. At full use that is US$25 per session, but it is not a like-for-like unit comparison: public [TestDome packs](https://www.testdome.com/pricing) list about $7–$20 per candidate, while [HackerRank](https://www.hackerrank.com/work/pricing) and [CodeSignal](https://codesignal.com/pricing/) list $20 overage attempts/credits and [CoderPad](https://coderpad.io/pricing/) lists $30 extra tests/interviews. Their included products, billing units, and service levels differ. This is for discovery only: do not publish the price or implement payment. Hosting, execution, and support costs are unmeasured; check them before offering the pilot. No willingness-to-pay, margin, or customer interview evidence is claimed.

### Buyer hypothesis and discovery questions

Initial buyer hypothesis: an engineering manager or technical founder sponsors the purchase because they own the engineering review burden; a recruiter operates invitations and candidate coordination. Competitors offer self-serve small-team tiers, which makes this segment worth investigating, but public pricing does not prove demand or buyer preference. No discovery interviews have been conducted.

Use these questions in future buyer discovery:

- How often do you review take-home engineering work, and how much engineering time does a typical review take? Where does review slow down?
- What evidence would make a work sample easier to discuss or act on: the final artifact, code changes, test runs, debugging steps, or an ordered timeline? What would not be useful?
- Who chooses the assessment, sends invitations, supports candidates, and owns the final evaluation in your team?
- Which parts of a candidate task create the most friction: setup, time burden, privacy/consent, accessibility, saving/resuming, or uncertainty about what happens next?
- What budget and approval path would a 30-day, one-scenario pilot need to fit, and what result would justify continuing? Ask without presenting the hypothesis price as an established market rate.

## Evidence limits

This is desk research from official vendor pages, not a hands-on product test or independent market study. It supports comparisons of published positioning, feature claims, candidate instructions, and listed prices. It does **not** establish comparative usability, visual polish, actual time-to-first-assessment, onboarding burden, reliability, assessment validity, candidate completion rates, commercial discounts, or product performance. Do not present vendor outcome claims as independently verified. Confirm plan entitlements, trial terms, candidate privacy requirements, and pricing with each vendor before a purchase comparison.
